Dear Editor,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript entitled “UGO-Omics: an expression-backed orthogroup resource for comparative underground-organ biology” for consideration as a Research Article in Molecular Plant.

Underground organs such as rhizomes, tubers, stolons, bulbs, corms, storage roots, and root crowns are central to plant persistence, regeneration, storage, and adaptation. However, molecular resources for these organs remain fragmented across species, organ types, and experimental systems. UGO-Omics addresses this gap by organizing public underground-organ transcriptomes into an expression-backed, orthogroup-centered comparative framework.

The manuscript presents three major advances. First, UGO-Omics integrates transcriptomic evidence, reference annotations, underground-organ expression scoring, cross-species orthogroup inference, candidate prioritization, benchmark recovery, and an interactive GitHub Pages platform with Global Search, UGO Score Explorer, Candidate Transfer, Orthogroup Detail, Species Browser, Candidate Expansion, Benchmark Recovery, and download layers. Second, the resource identifies conserved candidate modules associated with cell-wall remodeling, meristem and regrowth programs, developmental transcriptional regulation, hormone-related regulation, and stress resilience. Third, we show that prioritized candidates are transferable beyond the initial analytical core: a 9-species expanded OrthoFinder backbone contains 26,459 orthogroups, including 7,346 represented in all nine species, and all eight prioritized candidate orthogroups are remapped to the expanded backbone and remain traceable in newly added broad-layer species. A conservative known-module benchmark further recovers 11 of 15 curated underground-organ or storage-organ modules among prioritized candidates.

We believe this work will be of broad interest to the Molecular Plant readership because it provides both a reusable resource and a functional candidate-discovery and candidate-transfer framework for comparative plant developmental, regenerative, and storage-organ biology. The study is not positioned as functional validation of individual genes; rather, it provides a transparent, evidence-integrated system for prioritizing candidate orthogroups and generating testable hypotheses across underground-organ systems.

All manuscript-supporting materials, including the static website, JSON browser layers, figure source tables, orthogroup summaries, candidate expansion outputs, benchmark recovery tables, and public-ready release package, are available through a public repository (https://github.com/fenghuoranyale/UGO-Omics). The website is deployed as a static GitHub Pages platform (https://fenghuoranyale.github.io/UGO-Omics/), and a Zenodo DOI will be added before final publication.

This manuscript has not been published and is not under consideration elsewhere. All authors have approved the submission.
